Thesis

Scarcity at the core. Productive businesses around it. The trigger is one number: USDe supply.

What we're claiming

Ethena's dollar business already earns real money: $1.06B in lifetime fees and ~$245M over the last year. Until September none of that reached ENA. The Aug 27 package changed the structure: sellers bought out, investor unlocks ended in one release, a fee switch approved, and IP and residual value proposed to move to the Foundation.

What has to happen

USDe has to grow from $4.74B to $7.5B for the buyback to switch on, and keep going to $15B+ for buybacks to matter. That's the bet. For scale, the Foundation's stated ambition is more than $100B of USDe within five years. Target

What we're not claiming

That buybacks alone justify the price. At the first tier they're small (~$22.5M a year). The rerate comes from the market changing how it values ENA once the pipe is live.

The template

Hyperliquid is the template: HYPE is the network asset at the center; Trade[XYZ] is an equity company in orbit that holds licenses (like the S&P 500 deal) while its markets run on the network; its real-world relationships are moons. Fees route back to the network through automated HYPE purchases.

Why it fits Ethena

  • The sun: ENA, 15B max supply, with an approved fee switch and the proposed transfer of IP and residual value to the Foundation.
  • Planets: StablecoinX (Nasdaq-listed, holds ~20% of ENA); Ethena Labs, the operator; Ethena Pay; and white-label partners who brand their own dollars on Ethena's stack (Jupiter, MegaETH, ether.fi USD announced Oct 6).
  • Moons: Janus Henderson (ENA position, USDe cash management), the Aladdin integration, Coinbase and Robinhood channels, FalconX $1B facility, Binance stock basis, Anchorage issuing USDtb.

Two kinds of capital

Funds that can't hold tokens can buy the equity that holds them. StablecoinX (Nasdaq: USDE) listed June 26, 2026 and holds ~3.0B ENA, about 20% of supply. The proposed framework agreement assigns Ethena's IP and residual value to the Foundation, not Labs shareholders, so value concentrates in the token. Proposed

At ~$0.19, StablecoinX's ENA is worth ~$570M, against a market cap reported near $300M at its September high: roughly half of NAV. Its ENA is locked up and it carries equity and dilution risk, so the discount can persist. Read it as sentiment, not a free lunch.

The upper-K frame

Over a long enough timeframe, investors tend to lose money in the "bottom K" (no value accrual, unlock overhang), make some money in the "upper K" (real revenue, aligned tokens), and make the most in tokens graduating into it. Market participants are converging on what "quality" means, and marginal capital is flowing to strong teams with real revenue.

Why ENA fits "graduating"

  • Price marked $0.1516 on Sept 2; ~$0.19 on Oct 14, about +25% (it peaked near $0.30 in early October).
  • Still ~87% below its $1.52 high.
  • The remaining gaps (buybacks live, steadier revenue, growth) are the ones that would complete the move.

Caveat

The investor loop and upper-K framing come from market commentary, not data. They describe a pattern, not a guarantee.

05 · Revenue quality

Ethena is shifting from crypto funding toward steadier income.

Mix · CLOs · FalconX · stock basis

  • In early 2025, more than 80% of backing sat in crypto perp trades. Now about 80% sits outside crypto basis: DeFi and institutional lending, liquid stablecoins and RWAs. Blended yield 5.2%, against 7% on average since launch.
  • The trade-off: steadier income, lower peak yield. Non-cyclical revenue is what the upper K pays for.
For ENA

Less cyclical income means buyback cash that can survive a bear market.

New income streams

  • Stock basis: long tokenized US stocks, short equity perps on Binance. Equity-perp OI is above $2.9B and has grown at a 105% compound monthly rate this year. Equity basis averaged 3.56% annualized over six months. Live
  • Credit: Janus Henderson's AAA CLO strategy added to backing; the real-world-asset sleeve (JAAA, STAC) was ~$500M by June 17, 2026.
  • Institutional lending: the $1B FalconX facility, collateral at qualified custodians.

Why non-crypto-native revenue matters

The strongest revenue story is income that doesn't depend on crypto leverage, scales fast and isn't tied to the cycle. Equity basis and credit move Ethena in that direction.

Caveat

Steadier income has so far meant lower yield: sUSDe at 4.87% vs a 4.16% T-bill. Gross revenue fell from $151M (Q3 2025) to $65M (Q1 2026).

06 · The trigger clock

At today's run-rate, buybacks switch on in months, not years.

Supply and 30-day change · run-rate claim · trigger rule

  • USDe grew ~$570M in the last 30 days, mostly after token incentives ended. That's the answer to "growth was only bribed."
  • One catalyst nearly closes the gap: ~$90B of USDT sits on TRON, where USDe just launched. 3% of it is ~$2.7B.
Watch

If the 30-day pace holds above ~$500M, the first buyback lands by spring 2027.

The math

Gap: $7.5B − $4.74B = $2.76B. At $40M/day: ~69 days. At $30M/day: ~92 days. At the last 30 days' ~$19M/day: ~145 days. Compounding at the last 30 days' 13.7% gets there in ~3.6 months. The fee switch measures a 14-day average, so the switch flips about a week after the spot number crosses.

Catalysts that move the clock

  • TRON. USDT on TRON passed $90B. Much of it is low-velocity savings, where a yielding dollar beats a non-yielding one. USDe and sUSDe launched there in September. Live
  • Robinhood Chain. About $320M of USDe added in two months. Live
  • White-label. ether.fi USD ($300M+ of deposits that could convert), Jupiter, MegaETH. Launching
  • Equity perps. Equity-perp open interest passed $6B; Ethena expects equity basis to overtake crypto basis as its main yield source within 12–24 months. A trade.xyz (Hyperliquid) funding partnership is rumored, not confirmed. Rumor
  • Newer chains and DeFi. In the month to Oct 12, USDe grew +62% on Plasma, +41% on Mantle and +14% on Base. It is Morpho's largest dollar collateral, backing two of its three biggest markets.
  • Ethena Pay. Live in 48 countries, but no visible supply uplift yet.

Why "we just need one to hit" overstates it

The popular formula P(hit) = 1 − (1−p₁)(1−p₂)…(1−pₙ) assumes the bets are independent. These aren't: TRON demand, Robinhood flows, white-label launches and basis yield all rise and fall with the same crypto risk appetite and funding regime. In a bear market they tend to miss together. And a hit only counts if it is big: a $200M product doesn't move a $2.8B gap. Count the options, but size them.

Pushback on two popular claims

  • "Funding rates are boosting yield." Not lately: BTC basis on Binance fell to ~1% by September 2026. sUSDe's ~4–5% comes from lending, stablecoins and RWAs.
  • "The market prices zero value for $10B+." On a strict mature multiple, today's price already assumes USDe near ~$15B (see the rerate slide). The ratchet-ups are partly priced; the upside is beyond $15B.

For ENA

Ethena doesn't need to beat Tether. It needs a few percent of a market that keeps growing.

Three layers

  • Dollars onchain. ~$304B at the end of Q3: USDT ~$184B, USDC ~$75B, USDS ~$6.7B, USDe ~$4.9B (#4).
  • Dollars that pay. Yield-bearing stablecoins are ~5% of the market; the bank research expects that share to rise as users want yield as well as payments. Ethena is the #2 issuer behind Sky.
  • Assets to earn on. Tokenized RWAs went from ~$50B to $120B+ this year. The same research sees tokenized assets (stablecoins plus RWAs) rising from ~$350B to $4T by end-2028, and says RWAs and equity and commodity basis add ~$150T of potential collateral to ~$3T of crypto.

Regulation cuts both ways

The GENIUS Act stops US payment-stablecoin issuers from paying yield. That pushes yield-seeking demand toward products built for it, like USDe, but also limits where USDe can be distributed in the US.

Caveat

Stablecoin growth stalled this year: supply went sideways in Q3, and year-over-year growth was single digits. The $2T-by-2028 forecast needs roughly 6× from here; Citi's 2030 base case is $1.9T, bull $4T.

Versus the 2028 bank forecast

The bank's $2 by 2028 assumes $40B USDe, divides by ~8.9B tokens and counts the full take as buybacks. On 15B tokens with the 95% factor, its own math gives ~$1.09 for 2028. Our base case reaches $1.37–1.83 about three years later, with $50B USDe and burns.

For scale

  • $50B is about two-thirds of USDC today (~$75B).
  • $100B is Ethena's own five-year target, a bit over half of USDT.
  • Getting from $4.7B to $100B in five years means growing ~83% a year, roughly doubling annually, without incentives.

What has to be true

  1. Yield-bearing dollars gain share. The bank assumes only slightly faster growth than stablecoins overall; $100B needs much faster.
  2. Yield capacity keeps pace. Tokenized T-bills, credit, stocks and commodities give Ethena more to earn on.
  3. Ethena stays the default backend. White-label lets it earn on dollars it doesn't brand. Competitors: tokenized money funds, Sky, Paxos, Agora, Stripe/Bridge.
  4. Rates hold up. At 4% gross instead of 6%, every price above falls a third.
  5. The take rate doesn't stall growth. sUSDe has to keep beating the alternatives.

Not counted

White-label and Ethena Pay revenue is not added on top: in the bank's model it is what supports the take rate rising to 25%. Anything beyond that is upside. Burns are approximated at ~13B tokens; faster burns would lift every row.

Where the rerate comes from

Supply growth sets what the buybacks are worth. A rerate means the market paying up for that cash flow once it is visible and recurring, and pricing in the growth behind it. That's stage 2. No probabilities are assigned; this is a thesis, not a forecast.
